Privacy Policy — LoopOut

Last updated: 16 August 2026

LoopOut ("the app") counts the short-form videos you watch — Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Snapchat Spotlight and Facebook Reels — and helps you stay under a daily limit you set.

This policy describes exactly what the app accesses, what stays on your phone, and what is uploaded if you turn on cloud backup.

We do not sell your data, and the app contains no ads. The app does use Google Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics to understand which features are used and to receive crash reports. Analytics is on by default and can be switched off in Settings → Account → Share usage data.

Summary

  • The app works without an account. Used that way, none of your usage history leaves your phone, though anonymous app-usage analytics is still sent unless you switch it off.
  • Signing in with Google turns on cloud backup. Backup is part of having an account: if you sign in, your daily totals are uploaded, and there is no separate switch to keep the account while stopping the upload. If you do not want your totals uploaded, do not sign in — or delete your account, which removes everything.
  • When backup is on, the app uploads daily numeric totals only — a short, fixed list of numbers set out in full below.
  • Screen content is never uploaded. The Accessibility service reads on-screen information only to recognise that a short video was watched; that content is discarded immediately and only the resulting count is kept.
  • Friend battles are optional and off unless you turn them on. They are the only feature that shows any of your information to another person, and then only to friends you invited or whose code you entered — your name, your profile photo, and your daily video total. Nothing else.

What the app accesses on your device

On-screen content (Accessibility service)

To count videos, the app inspects the on-screen view hierarchy of a fixed list of apps, and no others:

  • Instagram (com.instagram.android)
  • YouTube (com.google.android.youtube)
  • TikTok (com.zhiliaoapp.musically)
  • Snapchat (com.snapchat.android)
  • Facebook (com.facebook.katana)

This list is enforced by Android itself, from the app's accessibility configuration. The system delivers the app nothing at all about any other app you use.

The service looks for the signature of a full-screen short-video player and increments a counter. It reads video captions and creator names only in memory, and only to tell one video from the next so the same video is not counted twice. That text is never written to storage, never logged, and never uploaded. Messages, photos, browsing history and the content of any app are never read, stored or transmitted.

You enable this service yourself in Android Settings, after an explicit in-app explanation, and can disable it at any time in Settings → Accessibility.

Screen-time statistics (Usage access)

With your permission, the app reads Android's own app-usage statistics to show how many minutes you spent in the tracked apps. This is queried live from the system each time it is displayed and is not stored in the app's database.

Apps you add yourself

You can add other installed apps to track their screen time. For those apps the app stores the package name and label, and reads screen time only. Their on-screen content is never inspected.

Step count (Physical activity)

One of the challenges you can pick to unlock reels is "walk a number of steps". If you choose it, the app reads your device's step counter only while that challenge is on screen, and only to count up to the number of steps needed to finish it.

To be exact about what this is and is not:

  • The step count is never written to the database, never backed up, never uploaded, and never shared with anyone — not with friends, not with us, not with any third party.
  • It is discarded the moment the challenge ends. Nothing about it survives closing the screen.
  • The app does not show you step totals, history, distance, calories, or any other fitness information, and it makes no health or fitness claims. The steps are a lock, not a feature.
  • The app does not connect to Health Connect, Google Fit, or any other health service, and it does not read heart rate or any body sensor.
  • The permission is optional. Deny it and the walk challenge is greyed out with the reason; every other challenge still works, including one that needs no sensor and no permission at all.

What is stored on your device

  • Daily counts of short videos watched, per platform
  • The daily limits you set, and any per-app time limits
  • Streak history and a derived "brain health" score
  • The package name and label of any app you add to tracking
  • Your settings: blocking mode, theme, counter size
  • If you set a parental PIN: a salted PBKDF2 hash of it. The PIN itself is never stored.
  • If you sign in: your email address, display name and profile-picture URL

This is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android Settings.

Cloud backup

Cloud backup is enabled automatically when you sign in with Google, and stays on for as long as the account exists. It is not a separate choice and there is no in-app switch to turn it off while remaining signed in.

Your control over it is the account itself. Not signing in means nothing is uploaded; the app is fully functional without an account. If you have signed in and want the uploads to stop, use Delete account and backed-up data in Settings → Account, which erases everything held on the server and signs you out.

Signing in

Sign-in uses Google. The app receives your Google account identifier, name, email and profile-picture URL. Your Google password is never seen by the app. Sign-in alone uploads no usage data — backup is a separate choice.

Your profile picture, when shown in the app, is loaded directly from Google's servers.

Exactly what is uploaded

If backup is on, the app uploads one row per completed day. Today is never uploaded; only days that have already ended. The complete list of fields is:

FieldWhat it is
user_idYour account identifier
dateThe calendar date the row is for
reels_instagram, reels_youtube, reels_tiktok, reels_snapchat, reels_facebookNumber of videos watched that day, per platform
limit_instagram, limit_youtube, limit_tiktok, limit_snapchat, limit_facebookThe daily limits you had set
minutes_instagram, minutes_youtube, minutes_tiktok, minutes_snapchat, minutes_facebookMinutes spent in each app — only for the most recently completed day
brain_health_scoreThe day's score, 0–100
under_limit, streak_dayWhether you stayed under your limit, and your streak length

That is the entire list. Nothing else is transmitted. In particular the app never uploads: screen content, video titles, captions, creator names, which specific videos you watched, the apps you added to tracking, your per-app limits, your settings, or your parental PIN.

Backup begins from the day you turn it on. Days before that are not uploaded.

Who processes it

Backup data is stored with Supabase (Supabase Inc.), which provides the database and the sign-in service. Data is held on Supabase infrastructure in Sydney, Australia (region ap-southeast-2). If you are outside Australia, turning on backup means the data listed above is transferred to and stored in Australia.

Access is enforced by database row-level security: backup rows are readable and writable only by the account that created them. (Friend battles, described below, are the one exception, and only for the specific fields listed there.) Data is transmitted over HTTPS.

Supabase's privacy policy: https://supabase.com/privacy

No other third party receives your data.

Friend battles (optional)

Friend battles are a daily leaderboard against people you invite: whoever watched fewer short videos that day is ahead. It is off unless you turn it on, and turning it on requires you to sign in, agree to cloud backup, and separately accept a screen that lists exactly what a friend will see.

Exactly what a friend can see

FieldWhat it is
display_nameYour name, taken from your Google account
avatar_urlYour Google profile photo
total_reels (per day)How many short videos you watched that day, as a single number

That is the entire list. A friend cannot see which apps you use, your per-platform breakdown, your screen time, your limits, your rules, your streaks, your brain-health score, or your email address. The daily backup row described above is not readable by anyone but you.

Who can see it

Only accounts you are mutually connected to. A connection is created in exactly one way: someone enters the six-character code the other person shared with them. There is no directory, no search by name or email, and no friend-of-friend visibility. Codes are random and are not derived from anything about you.

Turning it off

  • Remove a friend — on the Battle screen. The connection is deleted in both directions immediately, and neither of you can see the other again.
  • Leave battles — on the same screen. Stops sharing new daily totals.
  • Delete your account — removes your profile, all of your connections and every daily total you shared, along with everything else. See below.

Days you shared while battling remain in your account until you remove the friend or delete your account.

Turning backup off, and deleting your data

Analytics and crash reporting

The app uses Google Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics.

  • What is collected: which screens you open, which of the app's own features you use (for example that a challenge was started, completed or abandoned, and which permissions are granted), your device model, operating-system version, app version, country, and — for Crashlytics — the technical details of any crash.
  • Advertising ID. Firebase Analytics also collects your device's Android advertising ID. The app shows no ads and runs no ad campaigns; the ID is used only to keep analytics counts from treating one device as several. You can reset it, or stop apps receiving it entirely, in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. Switching off Share usage data in the app stops this along with everything else above.
  • What is never collected: your reel counts, screen time, streaks, video captions, creator names, or any on-screen content. Analytics carries no information about what you watched.
  • Analytics is on by default. You can switch it off at any time in Settings → Account → Share usage data; collection stops immediately.
  • Crash reports are always sent while the app is installed, because they contain only diagnostic information about failures and are what allows bugs to be fixed.

This data is processed by Google as part of the Firebase service and is used only to improve the app.

Push notifications

If you sign in, this device registers a push token with your account — a routing address that lets Google's messaging service deliver a notification to this phone. It is not usage data and says nothing about what you watch. It is used to tell you when a friend accepts a battle invite, and occasionally to send app news.

Not signing in means no token is ever registered. Deleting your account deletes the token with everything else. You can also turn these notifications off without touching your account, in your phone's notification settings. They are split into two channels so you can silence one and keep the other: Updates from LoopOut (news and tips from us, quiet by default) and Friend activity (when a friend accepts your invite).

Deleting your data

The account controls are in the app, under Settings → your account:

  • Sign out — ends your session on this device. Days already backed up stay in your account until you delete them.
  • Delete account and backed-up data — permanently deletes your account and every day you have backed up, along with your battle profile, all of your friend connections and every daily total you shared with them. This cannot be undone. Your on-device history is not affected; clear the app's storage in Android Settings if you want that gone too.

If you cannot reach the app, email mehdiaasar0@gmail.com from the address you signed in with and your account and backed-up data will be deleted.

How the data is used

Solely to run the app's features: counting videos, enforcing the limits you set, showing your stats and streaks, and — if you enabled them — backing up your daily totals to your own account and showing your daily total to friends you connected with. It is not used for advertising or profiling, and it is never sold. Apart from friend battles, which share only the three fields listed in that section and only with people you connected to yourself, your data is not shared with anyone.
